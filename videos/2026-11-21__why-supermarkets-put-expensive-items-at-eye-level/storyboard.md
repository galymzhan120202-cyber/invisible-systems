# Production Plan: Why Supermarkets Put Expensive Items at Eye Level

**System:** Visual merchandising and cognitive load optimization
**Narrator:** Bright, energetic adult female (en-US-AvaNeural)
**Accent Palette:** `signal blue` (the system/rules), `warm amber` (the shopper/desire), `coral red` (naive model/cost)
**Hero Element:** The supermarket aisle shelves (transforming), and the shopper's gaze.

| # | Time | Story beat | Stick-figure scene | Motion / camera / transition | English VO | SFX |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–10s | **YOU + NEED**: Shopper in a hurry. | A stick figure (warm amber) pushes a shopping cart through a generic supermarket aisle. Shelves are simple, filled with plain boxes. | Camera follows the shopper from behind, then moves to a slight side view. Cart wheels animate. | Every time you step into a supermarket, you have one mission: grab what you need and get out efficiently. **Continuity map:**
*   **Scene 1 → 2:** Shopper and cart continue their journey, then stop at a specific item.
*   **Scene 2 → 3:** The specific item grab transitions to the shopper observing patterns across multiple items.
*   **Scene 3 → 4:** The questioned pattern triggers the revelation of the underlying system, transforming the shelves.
*   **Scene 4 → 5:** The revealed system is then shown in action, demonstrating the cost.
*   **Scene 5 → 6:** The cost leads to the changed behavior of the shopper, now aware of the system. The zoned shelves from Scene 4 persist.
