# Production Plan: Why You Must Accept Cookies On Every Site

**System:** Compliance theater and distributed responsibility
**Narrator:** Bright, energetic adult female voice (e.g., AvaNeural)
**Palette:** Black canvas, white line-art; signal blue (system/rules), warm amber (you/desire), coral red (naive/wrong model)
**Hero Element:** A "Cookie" icon (starts as a simple circle, evolves into a data cluster, then a legal document fragment)

| # | Time | Story beat | Stick-figure scene | Motion / camera / transition | English VO | SFX |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–10s | **YOU + NEED** | A warm amber stick-figure "YOU" sits at a desk, looking at a simple white screen. A stylized "cookie" icon (a simple white circle) appears on the screen briefly before disappearing into the background. | Camera pushes in slightly on YOU. The screen loads content (subtle lines appearing). Cookie icon animates in and out smoothly. | You open a new website, ready to explore. All you want is a seamless experience, a quick digital journey. | `whoosh.mp3` (0.6s), `click-soft.mp3` (1.5s) |
| 2 | 10–20s | **GO** | The white screen is now mostly covered by a large, signal blue cookie banner. The "cookie" icon has transformed into a more complex white data cluster on the banner. YOU looks frustrated, hands raised. | The banner slides up quickly from the bottom, covering most of the screen. YOU recoils slightly. Camera subtly shakes. | But before you can begin, a huge banner slides up. “Accept Cookies?” it demands, blocking your view. This mandatory roadblock is on every site. | `whoosh-fast.mp3` (10.6s), `focus.mp3` (11.2s) |
| 3 | 20–30s | **SEARCH** | YOU points to a small, coral red "Decline" button (an X icon) on the banner, which quickly fades or is obscured by other elements. The data cluster "cookie" briefly flashes coral red. | YOU tries to interact with the small button, but it becomes difficult to reach or disappears, emphasizing its hidden nature. | You might assume this protects *your* privacy, giving *you* control. You look for a “no” button, but it's often hidden, making you wonder. | `focus.mp3` (20.6s), `stack-collapse.mp3` (25.4s) |
| 4 | 30–40s | **FIND** | The banner transforms. The "cookie" icon is now a fragment of a legal document (signal blue lines on white). The banner morphs into a simplified "Law" block (signal blue) next to YOU, who is now forced to tap a generic "Accept" button (warm amber highlight). | The banner's complex elements peel away, revealing simpler, blockier shapes representing legal structures. YOU's hand is guided to press the "Accept" button. | Regulations made companies *responsible* for asking. But instead of user-friendly choices, they shifted the burden to *you*. It's easier to make you click. | `transform.mp3` (30.6s), `click-soft.mp3` (32.1s) |
| 5 | 40–50s | **TAKE** | YOU sits, looking tired. A stack of tiny, identical "Accept" cookie banners (signal blue, with the legal document cookie icon) piles up behind YOU, getting taller and taller, representing wasted time. | The banners accumulate quickly and silently, emphasizing the repetitive, cumulative burden. YOU's head droops slightly. | What's the cost? Not your choice, but their compliance. You trade seconds for a performance, a forced interaction that rarely empowers. | `drop-thud.mp3` (40.6s), `whoosh.mp3` (44.5s) |
| 6 | 50–60s | **RETURN + CHANGE** | YOU is back at the desk, looking at a website. A new cookie banner appears, but YOU simply taps the "Accept" button (warm amber highlight) with quiet understanding. The legal document "cookie" icon is visible in the background, but less intrusive. | YOU performs the action smoothly, without frustration, but with a subtle nod of recognition. The camera pulls back slightly, showing YOU as part of a larger digital landscape. | Next time, you'll still click. Now you understand it's not a genuine dialogue. It’s an invisible system, a theatrical hurdle in your digital day. | `complete-done.mp3` (50.6s), `focus.mp3` (53.8s) |

**Continuity map:**
- **Scene 1 to 2:** YOU remains at the desk; the screen transitions from content to being covered by the cookie banner.
- **Scene 2 to 3:** YOU and the banner remain in place; YOU's focus shifts to the "Decline" option.
- **Scene 3 to 4:** YOU remains; the banner visually transforms to reveal its legal, system-driven nature.
- **Scene 4 to 5:** YOU remains; the single banner multiplies into a stack of identical banners, representing cumulative time cost.
- **Scene 5 to 6:** YOU remains at the desk; a new cookie banner appears, but YOU's reaction is different, showing newfound understanding.
