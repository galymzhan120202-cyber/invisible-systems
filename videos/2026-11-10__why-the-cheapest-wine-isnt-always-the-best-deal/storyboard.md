# Production Plan: Why The Cheapest Wine Isn't Always The Best Deal

*   **System:** Perceived value and loss aversion in pricing tiers.
*   **Narrator:** Bright, energetic adult female voice (e.g., AvaNeural).
*   **Palette:** Black canvas, white line art. Accent colors: warm amber (you/desire), coral red (naive model/social fear), signal blue (system revealed).
*   **Hero Element:** A stick-figure customer, a wine list icon, and various wine bottle icons.

| # | Time | Story beat | Stick-figure scene | Motion / camera / transition | English VO | SFX |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0–10s | **YOU + NEED**: Wanting a good deal. | A stick-figure customer (warm amber outline) sits at a table. A simple "wine list" icon (rectangle with lines) appears in front of them. The customer looks at the list, then looks up thoughtfully. | Camera focuses on the customer and the list. Subtle zoom on the list. Customer's head tilts slightly. | You're out for dinner, scanning the wine list. You want to pick something nice, but also be smart with your money. | `focus.mp3` (0.6s), `click-soft.mp3` (2.1s) |

The cheapest bottle seems like the obvious choice for value, a smart move. | `whoosh.mp3` (10.8s), `focus.mp3` (12.5s) |

**Continuity map:**
*   **Scene 1 to 2:** The customer and wine list persist. Customer's gaze becomes the focus.
*   **Scene 2 to 3:** The customer and the amber-highlighted cheapest bottle persist. The customer's internal thoughts become externalized.
*   **Scene 3 to 4:** The customer is implied, but the wine list transforms from simple display to a system diagram with the cheapest bottle as a reference point.
*   **Scene 4 to 5:** The system diagram (second-cheapest highlighted) persists. Focus shifts to the customer and the consequences (more money spent, restaurant profit).
*   **Scene 5 to 6:** The customer and the wine list return to their initial state, but with the subtle "system" overlay (blue lines) to show new understanding.
